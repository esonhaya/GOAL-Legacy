# Purpose

This document defines the structure and scope of the football universe simulated within GOAL: Legacy.

It establishes what exists in the world, how different football entities interact, and the guiding principles for expanding the simulation over time.

---

# The Football World

GOAL: Legacy simulates a living football ecosystem rather than a collection of isolated leagues.

Every club, player, manager, competition, nation, and governing body exists as part of one interconnected world.

Actions in one part of the world may influence another.

---

# Scope of the Simulation

The football universe includes:

- Nations
- Football associations
- Domestic leagues
- Domestic cups
- Continental competitions
- International competitions
- Professional clubs
- Youth academies
- Players
- Staff
- Referees (future)
- Agents (future)
- Media (future)
- Supporters (future)

Every addition should strengthen the existing ecosystem instead of existing independently.

---

# Connected World

No competition exists in isolation.

Examples:

- Players move between countries.
- Managers build careers across continents.
- Clubs scout internationally.
- International tournaments influence player value.
- Global trends affect local football.

The football world should always feel connected.

---

# Time

Time moves continuously across the entire simulation.

The world does not pause outside the player's club.

Examples:

- Other leagues continue playing.
- Transfers happen worldwide.
- Youth players develop.
- Clubs hire and dismiss managers.
- Competitions progress simultaneously.

The simulation exists independently of player observation.

---

# Club Identity

Every club should possess a unique identity.

Examples include:

- History
- Culture
- Club DNA
- Supporter expectations
- Rivalries
- Reputation
- Youth philosophy

No two clubs should feel identical over long-term saves.

---

# National Identity

Every football nation should gradually develop its own characteristics.

Examples:

- Tactical tendencies
- Youth development quality
- League reputation
- International success
- Football culture

National identities should evolve naturally through simulation.

---

# Competitions

Competitions represent the structure of organized football.

Examples:

- League championships
- Domestic cups
- Continental tournaments
- International tournaments

Competition rules remain data-driven and may differ between regions.

---

# World Evolution

The football universe changes continuously.

Examples:

- Clubs become stronger or weaker.
- Nations improve or decline.
- Tactical eras emerge.
- Financial power shifts.
- Legendary generations appear.
- Historic dynasties end.

The world should never remain static.

---

# Historical Continuity

The football universe remembers its past.

Examples:

- Champions
- Records
- Hall of Fame
- Historic rivalries
- Club legends
- Golden generations

History should enrich future gameplay.

---

# Scale

The architecture should support gradual expansion.

Phase 1 focuses on a stable football ecosystem.

Future versions may expand with:

- Women's football
- Regional competitions
- Amateur football
- Youth competitions
- Expanded international football
- Additional governing systems

Growth should occur without redesigning the existing universe.

---

# Design Principles

The football universe should always be:

- Persistent
- Connected
- Believable
- Dynamic
- Historically aware
- Simulation-first

---

# Locked Decisions

✓ The world exists beyond the player.

✓ Every club belongs to one global ecosystem.

✓ Football evolves continuously.

✓ History influences the future.

✓ Expansion should be modular.

✓ Authenticity takes priority over unnecessary complexity.
