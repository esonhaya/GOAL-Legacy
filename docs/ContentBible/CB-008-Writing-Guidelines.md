# Purpose

This document establishes the writing standards used throughout GOAL: Legacy.

Its purpose is to ensure consistency across documentation, UI text, articles, tooltips, tutorials, database content, and future localization efforts.

Every contributor should follow these guidelines.

---

# Writing Philosophy

Writing should be:

- Clear
- Consistent
- Professional
- Concise
- Football-focused
- Easy to understand

Avoid unnecessary complexity.

Simple writing is preferred over complicated writing.

---

# Tone

GOAL: Legacy should maintain a professional football tone.

Examples:

✓ "The club secured promotion."

✓ "The manager signed a new contract."

Avoid exaggerated or overly dramatic language unless intentionally written as part of fictional media.

---

# Terminology

Use consistent football terminology throughout the project.

Preferred examples:

- Club
- Manager
- Player
- Competition
- Match
- Squad
- Academy
- Transfer
- Contract
- Season

Avoid switching between multiple terms for the same concept.

---

# Capitalization

Capitalize official names.

Examples:

- Premier League
- UEFA Champions League
- Club World Cup

Generic references remain lowercase.

Examples:

- the league
- the club
- the manager

---

# Numbers

Write numbers consistently.

Examples:

- 25 players
- 3 seasons
- 18 clubs

Dates should follow ISO formatting within documentation when precision is required.

---

# Names

Use official names whenever available.

Avoid abbreviations unless they are commonly accepted.

Examples:

✓ Manchester United

✓ FC Barcelona

Avoid inconsistent shortening.

---

# User Interface

UI text should be:

- Short
- Direct
- Easy to scan

Examples:

✓ Save Game

✓ Continue

✓ Squad Registration

Avoid long sentences inside interface elements.

---

# News Writing

News should:

- Report events objectively
- Provide context
- Avoid unnecessary repetition
- Reflect the importance of the event

Different publication styles may be introduced in future versions.

---

# Documentation Style

Documentation should:

- Use Markdown
- Maintain consistent headings
- Use bullet lists where appropriate
- End with revision history
- Clearly define purpose and responsibilities

---

# Localization

Future translations should preserve meaning rather than literal wording.

Avoid idioms or culturally specific expressions unless intentionally required.

---

# Future Expansion

Future versions may define:

- Commentary writing
- Interview dialogue
- Press conference responses
- Tutorial writing
- Accessibility wording
- Regional language guidelines

These additions should extend the style guide without changing its core philosophy.

## P2-014 Matchday truth

Matchday copy is generated from canonical Match evidence. Use clear states such
as “Starting XI”, “Substitute appearance”, “Unused substitute”, “Not selected”,
and “Unavailable — injury or fitness”. Goal text may include minute, scorer,
assist, and score-after context only when those facts exist. Quiet Matches may
remain quiet; commentary must never invent shots, passes, saves, or chances.

Rating explanations are compact evidence summaries, with attacking, creative,
defensive, goalkeeping, and discipline language selected from the Player's
actual stat line. The controlled Player may receive bounded Player-of-the-Match
or decisive-contribution context, but these labels never grant direct attribute
boosts. World Matches keep compact fidelity and do not receive this storytelling
layer.

✓ Standardized terminology.
